Working Papers

AI Search as an Information Intermediary: Clickstream Evidence on Web Traffic

Qiaoni Shi, Kai Gu, and Kai Zhu

Paper

AI search retrieves web information to ground generated answers. We study this new information intermediary using U.S. desktop clickstream data observing use alongside referrals during ChatGPT Search’s rollout. Referrals accompany 5.4% of ChatGPT sessions versus 31.1% of Google queries. Ad-supported sites receive a smaller share of ChatGPT than Google referrals, while reference and technical sites receive larger shares. Conventional searches (Google, Bing, Yahoo) decline after ChatGPT adoption, sooner when adoption follows ChatGPT Search’s expansion to users without accounts. Among existing users, that expansion raises ChatGPT use; conventional searches show no detectable overall change but fall among households with lower prior search activity. These findings suggest AI search can reshape open-web traffic through its own referrals and changes in conventional search.

AI-Assisted Content Production on Multilingual Platforms

Kai Zhu, Kai Gu, and Lorenzo Lupo

Multilingual knowledge platforms serve readers of each language and maintain a stock of information across languages. We examine what AI-assisted translation contributes to each, comparing assisted and independently written Wikipedia articles in ten language editions against an English reference. In conditional comparisons, assisted articles contain about 27% more information because they transfer more of the English reference. They add about half as much information that the English reference lacks, so the pairwise knowledge stock is about 9% smaller. A reader of the target language finds more, and a reader or an AI system drawing on both languages finds less across the pair. These findings support evaluating AI-assisted production through both reader access and contributions to the knowledge stock.
