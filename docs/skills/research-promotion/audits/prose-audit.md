# Prose audit: reference corpus and evidence

Audit date: 2026-09-23. This is a qualitative close reading of a purposive reference
set, not a readability experiment or evidence about engagement. It supports the
editing decisions in [Prose editing](../guidance/prose-editing.md).

## Method and scope

Read each accessible text through five lenses: **W** words, **S** sentences,
**L** length variation, **P** paragraphs, and **T** pacing. Inspect the opening,
evidence/explanation, qualifications, and ending where present. Record a locator,
the observed choice, and a counterexample or limit. Derive an editing decision
separately; frequency or presence alone does not establish effectiveness.

The corpus contains three university articles, one licensed Economist article,
one AP feature, and two institutional LinkedIn posts. For Bocconi, paragraph
numbers count body prose after the standfirst, excluding headings. For X's
algorithm, use section-relative locators because the results include a list.
LinkedIn locators identify semantic blocks: extracted line breaks do not establish
the posts' visual paragraph layout. AP and Mint locators identify passage topics,
excluding navigation, advertisements, recommendations, and captions.

Do not infer detailed syntax or paragraph patterns from the X mirror examples or
inaccessible NYT material. Those are excluded from this audit. Previously inspected
agent skills are implementation precedents, not prose evidence. No new assessment
of the underlying studies' scientific validity is claimed here.

## R1 — University report: Goodreads

[Original article](https://www.unibocconi.it/en/news/chain-reaction-reshaped-goodreads).
Full body inspected.

- **W:** ¶4–6 connect participation, concentration, and diversity to publishers,
  campaigns, and genres. ¶5 expands HHI and defines its role; the single-use
  abbreviation, definition, and numerical comparison create a dense interruption.
- **S:** ¶4 moves from participation to campaign counts to smaller publishers.
  ¶7 uses a colon to specify the review/ratings contrast. ¶8 carries the preceding
  ratings into an explanation.
- **L:** ¶4 places a brief interpretation after the numerical result; ¶8 uses
  longer clauses to connect participation, matching, and ratings.
- **P:** ¶8 develops one explanation through several claims. Its unity comes from
  a governing mechanism, not from having only one fact.
- **T:** ¶4–9 move among affected groups and outcomes; transitions identify the
  shift. The final access image recalls the earlier participation problem.
- **Caution:** ¶3's superlative and ¶10's sweeping generalization need evidence;
  they are not models of precision. Dense index exposition could be simplified
  if readers need the interpretation rather than the index name.

## R2 — Author essay: question framing

[Original article](https://www.unibocconi.it/en/news/power-framing-questions).
Full body inspected.

- **W:** ¶1 establishes ordinary choices; ¶5–7 retain preferences, objectives,
  and randomization as useful names for the problem. AI is reused rather than
  accompanied by a succession of new abbreviations.
- **S:** ¶4 integrates aligned and conflicting objectives in a parallel conditional
  comparison. Under Randomization, the waiter illustration uses a colon to open
  the example's consequences.
- **L:** ¶3 briefly orients the reader before ¶4's longer reasoning. Length changes
  with function rather than following a repeating short/long pattern.
- **P:** The Controlling the Options paragraph combines user agency, platform
  control, and incomplete knowledge under one claim about constrained influence.
- **T:** The text proceeds from example to concept and back; the final restaurant
  callback gives earlier details a new explanatory function.
- **Caution:** Categorical claims under Randomization require the model's scope.
  The extended paragraph about questions could overload a shorter social post.

## R3 — University report: X's algorithm

[Original article](https://www.unibocconi.it/en/news/does-xs-algorithm-push-politics-right).
Full body and results list inspected.

- **W:** After the results list, partisan identity and affective polarization
  receive everyday explanations. The distinctions matter; replacing all of them
  with political change would erase the result's boundary.
- **S:** The results list uses parallel predicates. Under Why the asymmetry?,
  following new accounts becomes the starting point for the next consequence.
  The following paragraph's colon introduces an explanatory sequence.
- **L:** A brief conclusion follows the long author/affiliation sentence near the
  start. The latter's information load is an attribution problem, not a reason
  to prohibit long sentences generally.
- **P:** The paragraph immediately after the list groups a boundary, its plain
  explanation, and the contrast with the measured effects.
- **T:** Question subheads organize changing reader questions. The on/off distinction
  recurs with added mechanism and persistence claims.
- **Caution:** The ending repeatedly restates the contrast and raises a long-horizon
  question. Such repetition needs a new function; a question is not supporting
  evidence for extrapolation.

## R4 — Editorial analysis: remote work

[The Economist, licensed Mint republication](https://www.livemint.com/economy/the-working-from-home-illusion-fades-11689140290522.html).
Full body inspected; direct Economist access was unavailable.

- **W:** The collaboration passage introduces coordination costs after practical
  difficulties; the learning passage develops human capital. These abstract nouns
  organize explanations. Abbreviation burden is not a prominent feature here.
- **S:** The revised-study passage aligns earlier and revised estimates, then
  explains the changed data. The historical-study passage distinguishes hours
  worked from efficiency. Both make the comparison's basis consequential.
- **L:** A short correction about researcher error precedes a fuller explanation;
  longer sentences integrate two qualifications of the historical result.
- **P:** The revised-data paragraph supports its governing explanation with several
  observable service outcomes. The employee-preferences section changes the
  criterion for evaluating remote work.
- **T:** The revision changes direction; later, the welfare tradeoff slows a run
  of productivity findings and shifts the reader's question.
- **Caution:** Several studies arrive rapidly with different settings and measures.
  Compression can blur comparability. The polemical closing is a tone choice,
  not a general rule for university research news.

## R5 — Reported feature: productivity and automation

[AP original](https://apnews.com/article/inflation-pay-economy-productivity-workers-technology-prices-c1e0163d51b430fcb18f0c80e32b2d81).
Full body inspected.

- **W:** The productivity explanation retains the abstraction and specifies its measure.
- **S:** That passage uses a colon and modal qualifications; Reata's turnaround comparison aligns units.
- **L:** The Batesville opening alternates context with brief orientation.
- **P:** The productivity passage develops one mechanism through linked claims.
- **T:** Batesville leads to national context; Reata restores concrete action. Productivity vocabulary provides continuity.
- **Caution:** The AI-study introduction burdens attribution; magical metaphors do not themselves explain. Anecdotes do not establish the macroeconomic account.

## R6 — LinkedIn: visibility and careers

[Original post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/universita-bocconi_visibility-success-sci-activity-7332796765315739648-qKVK).
Main post and institutional link comment inspected.

- **W:** The opening defines SCI, which is reused. Visibility and recognition
  connect to hiring, promotion, and funding in the results block.
- **S:** A question leads to historical context and attribution; lengthy affiliations
  delay the answer. Career outcomes are parallel, with a later subgroup comparison.
- **L:** A short question and brief transition frame longer explanation. Attribution
  density matters independently of sentence length.
- **P:** Blocks move through setting, evidence/subgroups, and implications. The ending
  combines interpretation, quotation, generalization, and invitation.
- **T:** Repeated visibility vocabulary connects the argument; the subgroup turn adds
  substance. Visual paragraph spacing cannot be inferred from this extraction.
- **Caution:** A duplicated-tense error needs copy-editing. The ending extends toward
  algorithms beyond the reported setting; natural-experiment jargon and embedded
  hashtags add burden. The final block would benefit from separation or trimming.

## R7 — LinkedIn: perceptual disfluency

[Original post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/bocconi-marketing-department_consumerbehavior-behavioralscience-marketingresearch-activity-7422195439182995456-5WO1).
Main post and DOI comment inspected.

- **W:** A technical heading is followed by accessible font/background examples.
  Sample-size notation presumes research literacy.
- **S:** Familiar design choices lead to a question and attribution. The finding
  follows study scope; dash-delimited examples interrupt the cue/numerical comparison.
- **L:** Longer sentences integrate evidence and conditions, with little relief
  after the dense results block.
- **P:** The central block supports one finding through scale, examples, moderation,
  and boundaries. Some conditions require more explanation for a public audience.
- **T:** Concrete entry gives way to high conceptual density. The final general
  implication offers little additional explanation. Visual spacing is not verified.
- **Caution:** Correct attribution of disfluency is unexplained; the absence-of-choice
  boundary is difficult after a choice-focused opening. Practical implications are
  asserted without a specific action.

## Coverage and transfer to editing guidance

These are inferences from the observations, not additional claims about the sources.
Use the referenced decision IDs in [Prose editing](../guidance/prose-editing.md).

| Requested feature | Evidence locators above | Editing decisions |
|---|---|---|
| Precision | R3-W; R4-S | W1: preserve outcome distinctions and comparison bases |
| Technical vocabulary | R2-W; R3-W; R7-W | W2: retain, gloss, or translate according to reader need |
| Concrete verbs | R1-S; R2-W | W3: expose actors and actions without changing evidence strength |
| Useful abstract nouns | R1-W; R4-W; R6-W | W4: retain names that organize an explained relationship |
| Abbreviation burden | R1-W; R6-W; R7-W | W5: assess recognition, reuse, and learning cost |
| Information order | R1-S; R3-S | S1: attach new information to an available referent |
| Emphasis | R3-L; R4-L | S2: give the meaningful turn a clear main clause or ending |
| Parallel comparisons | R2-S; R3-S; R4-S; R5-S | S3: align syntax and substantive comparison dimensions |
| Punctuation | R1-S; R2-S; R3-S; R7-S | S4: show relationships; prevent interruptive nesting |
| Qualifications | R3-P; R4-S; R5-S; R7-P | S5: make the qualified claim and its boundary identifiable |
| Longer and shorter sentences | R2-L; R3-L; R4-L; R5-L | L1–L3: choose length by reasoning and orientation |
| Governing message and support | R1-P; R2-P; R3-P; R4-P; R5-P | P1: group distinct supporting claims under one message |
| Split, combine, or develop | R6-P; R7-P | P2–P4: repair changes of message, fragments, or missing links |
| Information density | R1-W; R3-L; R4-T; R7-T | T1: distribute new concepts, numbers, actors, and conditions |
| Transitions and breaks | R1-T; R3-T; R4-T; R5-T | T2–T3: mark a real relationship or shift in reader question |
| Purposeful repetition | R2-T; R3-T; R5-T; R6-T | T4: retain continuity and callbacks with a new function |

No numerical sentence or paragraph quota follows from this audit. The earlier
opening guide's word ranges remain flexible drafting aids. A limited corpus also
cannot establish a universal outlet style. Use the examples as choices with
reasons, including reasons to depart from them.
