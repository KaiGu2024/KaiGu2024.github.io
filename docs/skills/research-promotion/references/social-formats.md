# Social formats

Use these as editorial defaults, not platform rules or mandatory templates. Follow
the user's requested length and voice. Verify current official platform limits
when exact publication readiness depends on them; account tiers and counting
rules can change. Do not assume a character count from a word count.

## LinkedIn

Address a professional reader's decision or curiosity. Open with a concrete
tension, result, or recognizable situation; give the substantive payoff before
credits and publication details take over.

A useful arc is **situation or puzzle → finding → why to trust it → meaning for
this audience → boundary and paper link**. Rearrange it when the story benefits.
Aim for roughly 150–250 words unless the user specifies otherwise; shorter is
appropriate for one finding. Use readable paragraphs, with bullets only when
parallel findings are genuinely easier to scan.

Explain one decisive design detail in normal language. Name a relevant boundary
or subgroup rather than appending “more research is needed.” A managerial
implication should be phrased as a consideration to test unless the study actually
evaluates the proposed action. End with the paper, a useful question, or a practical
implication; generic invitations to agree add little.

Author posts may include a brief, authentic acknowledgement or excitement about
publication when supported by the brief. Institutional posts should credit the
research team without claiming ownership. Use hashtags or emojis sparingly when
they fit the speaker. Link directly by default; put links in a comment only if
requested. The examples in the source notes do not establish a reach benefit.

## X/Twitter: standalone post

Choose one finding or interpretive correction. Fit a meaningful contrast and enough
setting to prevent a false universal claim, followed by a route to the paper.
Compress the scaffolding, not the result's evidentiary strength. If the finding
cannot fit faithfully, propose a thread while still honoring any requested single
post by choosing a narrower claim.

For an unspecified short-post brief, a 260-character drafting budget leaves some
editing room; this is a house budget, not a statement of the platform's current
limit. Include the final URL and any numbering in the check. Exact X counting uses
platform-specific URL and Unicode weighting: use a current compatible validator
if available, or label a plain-character count as approximate and leave headroom.
Do not silently assume a subscription allows a longer post.

## X/Twitter: thread

Design one argument as a sequence; do not cut a long article into arbitrary pieces.
A typical short research thread needs five to seven posts, but obey the requested
count and avoid filler. Possible sequence:

1. A self-contained hook with the finding or honest promise of what readers will learn.
2. The concrete problem and comparison the researchers could observe or test.
3. The main finding, with an intelligible magnitude if available.
4. The mechanism or surprising second result, labeled at its actual evidence strength.
5. The boundary, null finding, or alternate interpretation that shapes the lesson.
6. The useful implication, authors/publication status, and paper link.

Combine or reorder beats as needed. Each post should contribute a new piece of the
explanation. Preserve essential scope in the opening because it may be reshared
alone. Keep qualifications next to the claims they constrain. Number consistently
if useful and count the numbers as text. A source link can appear early when
credibility is central and again at the end if useful; do not hide it to manufacture
suspense. Avoid obligatory “a thread” announcements and follow/reshare requests.

## Meta Threads

Distinguish the app from a connected thread. Default to a single conversational
post if the request is “post on Threads.” Use one accessible observation, the
research finding, and a natural invitation to think about it; a question is optional.
A house drafting budget of 450 characters encourages brevity while leaving editing
room, but verify current platform limits when relevant. A multi-post sequence
should be requested or substantively justified, not inferred from the app's name.

## Worked transformation: invented teaching example

The following facts are synthetic, not a real publication: a preregistered trial
at one online retailer randomly assigned 2,400 customers to receive a reminder or
no reminder. Purchases within seven days were 12% versus 10%, a 2-percentage-point
increase; the reported 95% interval for the difference was 0.2–3.8 points. Basket
value did not detectably change. The reminder contained no discount. The trial
does not isolate a psychological mechanism and has no long-term outcomes.

- **Academic phrasing:** “Reminder assignment increased seven-day purchase
  incidence by 2 percentage points.”
- **Short-post opening:** “A reminder without a discount nudged more shoppers to
  buy. In one retailer's randomized trial, 12% purchased within a week, versus 10%
  without the reminder.”
- **LinkedIn opening:** “Does bringing shoppers back require another discount?
  At one online retailer, a simple reminder increased the share who bought within
  a week from 10% to 12%. The message offered no price cut.”
- **Unfaithful escalation:** “One message boosts sales by 20% and builds loyal
  customers.” This substitutes sales for purchase incidence, omits setting, and
  invents loyalty. A relative increase must identify its outcome and baseline.

The stronger openings supply an everyday decision and reveal the result; neither
needs to invent a breakthrough or a personal anecdote.
