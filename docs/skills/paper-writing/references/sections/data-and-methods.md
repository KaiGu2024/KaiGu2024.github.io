# Data and Methods

Explain how the research question becomes an empirical investigation: how the setting generates observations and variation, how the data represent the constructs, and how the analysis supports an answer. Readers should understand both what is being compared and what that comparison can establish.

## Develop the empirical account

### 1. Explain the setting and relevant process

Introduce the actors, activities, institutional rules, and sequence of events needed to understand the investigation. Select background because it explains behavior, observation, or variation. Show how the setting instantiates the question; a history of the organization or industry belongs here only when consequential.

For an intervention or institutional change, explain what changed, for whom, when, and what the comparison group experienced. Distinguish assignment, eligibility, actual exposure, and take-up when they differ. Explain what participants knew if that affects their response. A process diagram or timeline can replace a difficult verbal account when it clarifies these relationships.

### 2. Explain how the data become the analysis sample

State sources, coverage, collection period, and the population from which observations arise. Describe sampling or recruitment and the consequential steps from available records to the analysis sample: linkage, exclusions, missingness, and attrition. Give sample counts at transitions that readers need to reconcile later analyses. Distinguish units of assignment, observation, and analysis, including repeated observations or aggregation.

Explain the role of each source in answering the question. When sources complement one another, make their coverage and connection clear. Relate measurement and follow-up windows to the event or intervention; the calendar span alone may conceal different exposure durations. Explain differing samples across outcomes where those differences affect comparisons.

Explain what the data let the study observe and compare. Develop a new capability when it is central to the contribution.

### 3. Connect constructs to empirical variables

Starting from the [conceptual framework](conceptual-framework.md), explain what each central measure captures and how it is constructed. Give consequential units, denominators, timing, aggregation, thresholds, and transformations. Develop the rationale for choices that affect meaning; place routine variable definitions in a compact table or appendix.

Explain the link between an intervention and the construct it is intended to influence, or between a proxy and the phenomenon it represents. If several measures represent one construct, explain their complementary roles. For indices, text measures, or algorithmic scores, describe inputs, aggregation, and validation so readers understand what the measure captures.

### 4. Use descriptive evidence to orient the reader

Select sample characteristics, distributions, and displays that establish context for the analysis: who is represented, how much relevant variation exists, or why a measurement or specification choice matters. Explain the substantive pattern rather than narrating every cell of a summary table. Distinguish sample description from design diagnostics and findings that answer the research question; the last may open Results.

Place diagnostics near the issue they assess. Explain what baseline balance or assignment checks reveal about implementation and any discrepancy that affects later comparisons.

### 5. State the comparison and its inferential basis

State the empirical target in substantive terms before technical detail: the outcome or relationship, the population and period, and the contrast or benchmark. Distinguish descriptive, predictive, causal, and model-based objectives. When analyses are compared, establish whether they address the same target, sample, and outcome definition; explain any consequential difference.

For causal claims, connect the source of variation to the comparison and explain why it identifies the effect of interest. Address the assumptions and threats that affect that reasoning. If the analysis selects participants by realized exposure or behavior after assignment, explain how that selection affects the comparison.

### 6. Explain estimation and inference as an implementation of the comparison

Introduce each principal specification by its purpose. When an equation helps, define its terms and explain which coefficient, contrast, or combination answers the question. Explain consequential controls, fixed effects, weights, and sample restrictions through their roles. Connect the reported quantity to the fitted model when it requires aggregation, normalization, or a nonlinear calculation.

Explain uncertainty procedures in relation to assignment, repeated observations, or other relevant dependence. Introduce alternative specifications by the concern they examine. Report diagnostic findings where they are needed to assess the design; broader sensitivity results can accompany the findings they qualify. Standard methods can be cited with the study-specific implementation; novel methods need enough development to understand their operation and purpose.

For predictive work, explain the evaluation population, information available at prediction time, validation scheme, and performance benchmark. For structural work, connect the model to observed behavior, the variation or moments informing its parameters, and the counterfactuals it supports.

## Organization and boundaries

Choose an order that resolves dependencies for readers. Setting and design can precede data when they explain how observations arise. Pair data sources with their specifications, or introduce a distinct analysis's method immediately before its results.

Keep decisions necessary to understand the central claims in the main text. Defer exhaustive implementation, codebooks, and supporting checks to appendix references. Include applicable ethics and registration information, with departures that affect the account.

## Grounding and handoff

Draft directly from the data documentation, code, outputs, and design records. Resolve conflicting records with a [claim-to-source check](../review-checks.md#claim-to-source-check) when needed; ordinary drafting can use the sources without a separate verification workflow.
