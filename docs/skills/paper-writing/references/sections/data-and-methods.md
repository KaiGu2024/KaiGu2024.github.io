# Data and Methods

Explain how the conceptual question becomes an empirical investigation: what is observed, how the constructs are represented, what comparison or target quantity is studied, and what the design and analysis can establish. This is a baseline writing guide, not a prescribed design or an audited journal template. Data, setting, measurement, identification, and estimation may be separate sections when their development warrants it.

## Develop the empirical account

1. **Setting and observation.** Give the institutional facts needed to understand behavior, observation, and relevant variation. State data sources, coverage, period, unit of observation, and sampling or recruitment. Explain consequential selection, exclusions, missingness, linkage, and attrition using documented records. Clarify what the data cannot observe when that limits the question.
2. **Constructs and measures.** Connect each central construct to its operational definition, manipulation, or measure. State units, denominators, timing, aggregation, and comparison categories where relevant. Explain consequential measurement choices and available validation; a proxy does not become the construct merely because it shares its name. Keep conceptual definitions consistent with the framework.
3. **Question, comparison, and design.** State the empirical target and comparison in substantive terms before technical detail. Distinguish descriptive, predictive, causal, and model-based objectives. Where causal interpretation is intended, explain the source of variation, identifying assumptions, and material threats. A named estimator or rich dataset is not an identification argument.
4. **Estimation and inference.** Explain how the implemented analysis estimates or evaluates that target. Define equations and connect their terms to the substantive comparison. Report sample restrictions, transformations, controls, fixed effects, weights, and uncertainty procedures when used and consequential. For structural or predictive work, explain the relevant estimation, calibration, validation, or evaluation logic without forcing a reduced-form template.
5. **Scope and supporting detail.** Keep decisions necessary to judge the central claims in the main text. Defer exhaustive implementation, codebooks, and supporting checks to precise appendix references. Report ethics or registration information when applicable and documented; distinguish planned from exploratory analysis without inventing an analysis history.

These are explanatory jobs, not five required paragraphs or a fixed subsection order. Make each methodological choice intelligible in relation to the question it helps answer. If new data or measurement capabilities overcome a prior obstacle, explain the capability specifically and support the comparison with earlier studies.

## Grounding and handoff

Draft from the actual data documentation, code, outputs, and design records. Use a [claim-to-source check](../review-checks.md#claim-to-source-check) when drafting directly from these artifacts or resolving discrepancies. Identify missing details that matter to the account; keep a separate provenance record when the task calls for one.

End with enough understanding for the reader to interpret the comparisons in [Results](results.md). Preserve limits on the target population, construct coverage, and inference in later findings. The appendix skill owns the support audit; this guide owns the main-text explanation.
