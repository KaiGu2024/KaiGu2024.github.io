# Conceptual Framework

This is initial guidance on the section's purpose and boundaries; detailed paragraph development and an empirical-paper audit can be developed later. Do not present this outline as a convention verified against the three introduction exemplars.

## Purpose

Help readers understand and position the study's central constructs and theoretical relationships through the relevant fields of literature. A construct is a high-level abstraction of the empirical objects or processes studied. Theory explains relationships among constructs: why a relationship should arise, under which assumptions, and within what scope. Naming a relationship or showing a correlation alone does not provide that explanation.

Use the literatures to develop a coherent account of the study's question. Establish how relevant traditions define the constructs, explain their relationships, and differ in assumptions or emphasis. Connect those perspectives where doing so clarifies the study; distinguish meanings that cannot be combined. Do not require several literature fields when one supplies the relevant conceptual foundation.

## Initial development principles

- Define and distinguish the constructs needed for the question, keeping the [shared construct lock](../main-text.md#within-paper-construct-lock). Explain consequential differences between the study's use and established uses.
- Synthesize prior theory and evidence around the conceptual relationships. Organize by the reasoning readers need, with literature strands contributing to that reasoning; avoid a sequence of disconnected summaries of individual papers.
- State the account the study adopts, combines, extends, or questions. Explain why that choice suits the phenomenon, including assumptions, competing explanations, or boundary conditions when relevant. An empirical paper need not claim a new theory.
- Connect the reasoning to the distinctions or questions the empirical analysis will investigate. Predictions, competing predictions, or numbered hypotheses are optional and must follow from supported reasoning. Preserve genuine indeterminacy and distinguish exploratory interpretations from advance predictions.

## Boundaries with other sections and skills

The [introduction](introduction.md) previews the question and positions the paper's contribution. Here, literature positioning explains the meaning of the constructs and the logic of the theoretical account in enough depth to support the investigation. Avoid repeating the introduction's contribution claims.

[Data and Methods](data-and-methods.md) explains how the concepts are observed and what the design identifies. This section can signal that connection without replacing measurement and identification details. Results and Discussion assess the evidence and its implications; do not write backward from findings as though every interpretation had been predicted.

Placement as Section 2 is an option, not a requirement. Institutional background may need to precede the framework; conceptual development may be integrated with background or relevant literature when a separate section adds little.

The separate conceptual-framework skill creates a figure from a supplied paper. It can support this section when a diagram clarifies the argument, but writing the section does not require a figure or invoke that skill automatically.
