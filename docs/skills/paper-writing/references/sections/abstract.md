# Abstract

## Compress the argument, not the paper

Treat the abstract as the paper's intellectual argument in miniature, not one sentence per manuscript section. Finalize it after the findings and framing stabilize; an earlier working abstract can help clarify the argument.

Use the author's supplied word budget. For submission-ready work, verify the target journal's current abstract limit and format from official author guidance; flag any conflict with the requested budget. If no venue or budget is given, produce a concise draft without claiming a universal limit. If guidance cannot be accessed, state that the limit is unverified and proceed with a clearly provisional budget. Do not impose five sentences or 150 words unless explicitly required.

These are **five rhetorical functions, not five mandatory sentences or separately labeled parts**. Combine, reorder, or omit conditional functions when the argument warrants it. Allocate space to the paper's contribution: a measurement comparison may need substantial method and benchmark detail; a substantive tension may be clear with a brief design description.

1. **Problem and tension.** Make the substantive question, unresolved distinction, puzzle, constraint, or decision legible. Where the argument needs conceptual framing, name the central construct—the high-level abstraction represented by the empirical objects—and the relationship that makes the question matter. Draw on theory to explain that relationship and why it is expected, uncertain, or conditional. Problematize a consequential unresolved inference, assumption, or boundary condition rather than merely announcing that few studies exist. Prefer the paper's specific problem to an opening about a technology's growing importance. Do not invent a paradox or opposing effects for a paper whose contribution does not require them.
2. **Empirical object and answerability.** Connect the conceptual question to its empirical realization: identify the setting, comparison, and design needed to understand what the evidence represents. When it matters to the contribution, explain how the study addresses the obstacle named in the opening—for example, through an informative benchmark, relevant variation, or previously unavailable observation. Let sample scale or data richness establish that opportunity rather than merely advertise size. Keep the construct, its manifestation, and its operational measure distinct; retain a measurement boundary when it limits the claim. Name methods when their comparison is the question; otherwise compress implementation detail. Retain what distinguishes descriptive, causal, and model-based evidence.
3. **Central finding or connected findings.** State the substantive answer, with quantitative anchors when they help convey its magnitude or comparison. A precise qualitative answer can suffice. When several results carry the contribution, make their relationship explicit: direct response and wider consequence, benefit and offsetting cost, or relative improvement and remaining inadequacy. These are possible logics, not required patterns. Give the unit and benchmark needed to interpret a number; several matched magnitudes can be useful in a comparative paper. Retain uncertainty when it changes the takeaway. Organize around what the results jointly establish, not table order.
4. **Interpretation and informative extensions.** Include a boundary condition, mechanism, characterization, institutional comparison, or simulation only when it materially changes the headline's interpretation. Heterogeneity is not automatically a mechanism; model-implied outcomes are not observed effects. Omit this move if unsupported or unnecessary.
5. **Contribution and closure.** Make clear what the study changes in understanding, evaluation, or decision making. This can be accomplished by the central finding itself, an interpretive framework, a bounded implication, or a concrete application; a separate significance sentence is optional. Close the question opened at the start. A secondary analytical or practical extension may be previewed when its purpose is informative, without claiming that a proposed remedy was tested. "Provides new insights" or "has important implications" adds little unless the precise object follows. Do not turn a measured distinction into an unsupported welfare ranking or policy prescription.

The [introduction](introduction.md) develops constructs, relationships, and problematization across paragraphs; the abstract compresses only the reasoning needed to understand the study. The conceptual problem and empirical object can share a sentence. Do not reproduce the full introduction opening, require an abstract definition of a familiar construct, or invent a theoretical contribution for a descriptive study.

The abstract gives readers a self-contained question, empirical basis, and answer. Explain an unfamiliar central construct or comparison briefly enough to understand that answer; avoid reproducing the literature positioning or roadmap from the introduction. Detailed evidence and analysis belong in [Results](results.md). Numerical precision is useful when it changes understanding, not as a mandatory slot.

## Select evidence by its job

Before compressing, classify the available findings internally. This is a priority guide, not a quota or an instruction to manufacture missing analyses.

| Role | Abstract treatment |
|---|---|
| Headline | State the central finding or decomposition directly. |
| Explanatory | Include supported evidence needed to understand why, where, or for whom the headline holds; distinguish a tested mechanism from an interpretation. |
| Interpretive extension | Include selectively if it changes the theoretical or managerial takeaway. |
| Corroborative | Usually fold into the headline clause, preserving any different sample or measurement scope. |
| Robustness | Usually omit from the abstract; retain when it materially qualifies credibility or is itself the contribution. |

Assign roles by the paper's claim, not by method name. A simulation can be the headline in a model-based paper; validation can be central in a measurement paper. Use a deletion test: if removing an analysis leaves the central interpretation unchanged, it probably does not need its own abstract sentence. Material contradictory evidence is not disposable "robustness."

## When components change the aggregate's interpretation

When the contribution is that an aggregate masks opposing components, foreground those components and explain the net outcome as their consequence. Do not automatically headline the aggregate merely because it has the largest or most familiar number. Conversely, do not bury an aggregate that is the author's actual central question.

An accounting identity such as `Y = A + B` requires compatible units, populations, and definitions, with exhaustive, nonoverlapping components. Estimated component changes need the same estimand and compatible estimation to reconcile; do not add estimates from different samples or specifications. Conceptual contrasts such as quantity versus quality are not automatically additive identities, and opposing movements alone do not identify a causal tradeoff.

Apply the [construct lock](../main-text.md#within-paper-construct-lock) before drafting, then the evidence hierarchy here, then the [substantive-compression rules](../main-text.md#substantive-compression-and-local-qualification). Deliver the requested abstract, not the internal planning apparatus, unless the author asks for it.
