# Main Text — shared drafting guidance

Use this file for substantive development and [academic voice](academic-voice.md) for prose. Load the relevant section guide below. The guides describe argumentative functions: adapt their order, length, and paragraph allocation to the project. Source audits document their basis; they are optional background, not drafting steps.

## Section guides

| Guide | Purpose |
|---|---|

## Develop the argument

Identify the passage's central claim and arrange the reasoning so each step prepares the next. Include a detail when it helps answer the question, explains the reasoning, makes a comparison interpretable, or changes an implication. Select findings by their [role in the argument](sections/abstract.md#select-evidence-by-its-job). A working draft can help develop the argument before analysis is complete.

Work from the supplied manuscript, literature, analysis outputs, or research notes. If an essential input is missing, identify it briefly and continue with the available material; use placeholders in unfinished drafts.

## Motivate section and subsection openings

At the start of every substantive section and subsection, establish what the reader is about to learn and why that matters to the paper's argument. Give this local motivation before empirical techniques, table references, or findings. It can identify an unresolved question, a distinction that changes interpretation, a theoretical prediction, a practical decision, or a concern about the preceding evidence. In a data or methods section, explain what must be observed or distinguished to answer the question before describing the data or estimator. In Discussion, reconnect to the question whose implications now need interpretation.

Keep the setup proportional: often one or two sentences, or a short purpose clause, are enough. If the preceding transition already establishes both the question and its stakes, carry it forward without repeating it. An umbrella section that immediately introduces subsections need not duplicate their motivation. This is a reader-orientation rule, not a requirement for a new introduction under every heading; abstracts retain their own compressed structure.

Make the reason specific. Announcing that the section presents results or uses a technique does not explain why the analysis is needed. Explain what answering the question will clarify, without inventing theoretical predictions or retrospectively presenting exploratory work as planned. Once the purpose is clear, move promptly to the approach, evidence, and interpretation. See [Results](sections/results.md#open-with-the-question-and-its-stakes) for the empirical sequence and [source examples](evidence/section-opening-examples.md) for published illustrations.

During revision, read each opening with its preceding transition: can a reader identify both the local question and why its answer matters before encountering the procedure or result? If not, repair that connection; if so, avoid adding generic motivation.

## Section and subsection titles

Organize sections and subsections around the substantive questions they address. Use simple, informative titles that identify the subject of inquiry, letting the prose establish the findings, interpretation, and limits. Short descriptive phrases are a useful default; a question can guide the organization without appearing as a literal question in the heading. In Results, an outcome, group, relationship, or process can name the inquiry when it fits. One question may connect several outcomes.

Use technique names in Methods or when an unusual or distinctive method is itself the subject of the section. Familiar major-section labels can provide clear navigation, with more specific titles beneath them. Keep sibling headings reasonably parallel in grammar and level of detail, and add subsections only when they mark distinct questions. Follow the manuscript's established formatting and the target venue's requirements for capitalization, numbering, and heading levels.

Read the headings together to check that they convey a coherent progression. See [real-paper heading examples](evidence/section-title-examples.md) for optional comparisons, and [Discussion](sections/discussion.md#choose-the-sections-work-and-shape) for its continuous-section structure.

For figure titles, captions, and panel subtitles, use [Results: exhibit titles](sections/results.md#exhibit-titles-and-panel-subtitles). Exhibit wording should identify the displayed quantity and comparison without making a stronger claim than the evidence supports.

## Keep constructs consistent

Use central terms consistently, distinguishing a construct from its empirical manifestation and measure. When related terms are easily confused, clarify their meanings and shorthand before revising the prose.

Use established terminology when it fits, and define deliberate extensions or new constructs. Resolve routine inconsistencies directly. A [terminology check](review-checks.md#terminology-check) can help when competing meanings affect the argument.

## State the answer directly

State what the analysis establishes. Combine corroborating evidence with the finding it supports and remove procedural narration that adds no understanding.

Establish scope and assumptions where readers first need them, then let later statements use that context. Add or repeat a qualification when omitting it would change the answer, its credibility, or its use. A precise noun, comparison, or verb can carry the distinction without an extra caveat sentence. Avoid attaching a list of limitations to every finding or implication; methods and discussion can carry the fuller account.

Compression should retain the claim's meaning: association, an estimated effect, and a modeled outcome are different answers. Keep numerical comparisons interpretable and distinguish findings from proposed explanations. [Results](sections/results.md) covers estimates and uncertainty; [academic voice](academic-voice.md#preserve-the-evidence) covers meaning during prose revision.

## Targeted checks

Use supplied sources directly during drafting. Turn to [review checks](review-checks.md) for conflicting evidence, consequential uncertainty about a source or term, or a requested audit. Return the requested text or critique, mentioning unresolved issues that affect it.
