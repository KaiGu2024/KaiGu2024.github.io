# Academic-voice audit: three recognized empirical papers

Audited 2026-09-22 to refine [academic-voice.md](academic-voice.md). These are the same three papers used in the [introduction audit](introduction-three-paper-audit.md), which records verified bibliographic metadata, recognition, exact versions, introduction paragraph labels, and PDF checksums. This follow-up examines prose choices rather than prescribing paragraph order. The recommendations are editorial inferences from selected passages, not measured journal frequencies or evidence that these choices caused publication success.

## Observations and resulting edits

The introduction labels E, M, and I refer to the earlier audit. Summaries below paraphrase the passages; they are not model sentences to reuse in unrelated projects.

| Prose feature | Evidence in the sampled passages | Implication for the skill |
|---|---|---|
| Subjects reflect the claim's role | E3–E4 describe author actions; E6–E8 shift to enterprises, households, and prices. M8 alternates the authors' finding with the methods' performance. I5 alternates platform actions and author findings. | Preserve that functional variation. The author's impersonal-subject preference should not become a rule against “we.” |
| Confidence changes with the inference | E6 and E12 distinguish patterns from an explicitly speculative capacity explanation. M11 and the heterogeneity discussion on p. 38 mark author interpretations as conjectures. I6 states findings; §6 distinguishes them from unresolved long-term consequences. | State supported results directly and qualify explanations locally. Avoid both blanket hedging and an unmarked jump from result to mechanism. |
| Syntax makes comparisons legible | M8 grants one method's relative improvement before stressing its remaining error. I5 places hiring benefits beside disintermediation costs. E8 separates statistical precision from economic magnitude. | Keep the relevant comparator and concession close to the finding. Use parallel clauses where they reduce the burden of comparison. |
| Technical clarity does not require uniform formality | M3 explains the ad-delivery process before its identification problem; M6 supplies an intuitive account of an assumption. I4 explains trust through services users need less. E12 illustrates a production constraint concretely. | Explain the unfamiliar link while retaining precise terms. Neither extra notation nor exclusively abstract nouns are prerequisites for academic voice. |
| Evidence supports an interpretive step | Egger §4.1, p. 2618, compares expenditure estimates and explains their relevance to spillovers. Gu and Zhu §4.1, p. 7, turn the hiring/revenue contrast into a question for §4.2. Gordon §5.3, p. 31, explains why a relative-error measure needs a complementary absolute-error measure. | Make the reader's next inference explicit. A locator helps inspect evidence; a list of columns alone does not explain its significance. |
| Repetition changes function across sections | The introductions preview central findings; the sampled results attach evidence; the conclusions revisit what those findings establish. Their repeated terms retain recognizable objects of comparison. | Preserve deliberate continuity while removing repetition that serves no additional purpose. Do not pursue compression through synonym substitution or deletion of every repeated finding. |
| Limits specify what cannot be inferred | Egger's conclusion discusses conditions affecting transportability. Gu and Zhu's §6 distinguishes observed short-term effects from opposing longer-term possibilities. Gordon §7.1 treats better data infrastructure as a prospective route needing further evidence. | Make a limitation govern a particular inference or decision. Retain the distinction between an empirical result and a proposed remedy. |

## What not to imitate mechanically

- **First person and tense:** all three use first person, but three papers do not establish venue-wide preferences. Tense varies with author actions, empirical summaries, and general relationships; do not standardize it by journal label.
- **Table narration:** Gordon §5.3 and Gu and Zhu §4.2 include substantial display-oriented exposition. Orientation can help, but the skill should retain its preference for a substantive comparison and decisive evidence over a column tour.
- **Numerical phrasing:** Gordon's p. 31 moves among ratios, percentage error, and multiplicative language; Gu and Zhu's p. 7 uses percent notation around changes in hiring shares. These are reasons to verify denominators and units, not wording conventions to adopt. This audit does not adjudicate or revise their estimates.
- **Strength of wording:** phrases claiming confirmation, broad novelty, or absence of an effect need their own evidentiary support. Recognition of a paper does not make every such formulation transferable. A voice-only edit must flag questionable inference rather than silently strengthen or weaken it.

The audit supports practical choices about agency, comparisons, explanation, and confidence. It does not justify new phrase blacklists, sentence-length quotas, a mandatory question in every results section, or separate house-style profiles from one paper per venue.
