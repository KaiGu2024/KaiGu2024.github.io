## Implications for the skill

These are editorial inferences from the maps, not journal requirements.

| Guidance under review | Assessment | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Conceptual motivation followed by a research-question bridge | Compatible with E1–E3, M1–M4, and I1–I4; question and study preview can share a paragraph. | Keep the bridge's function explicit; do not treat a standalone paragraph or interrogative syntax as a quality criterion. |
| Explain the unresolved question | Incomplete without distinguishing a knowledge gap from an observation, measurement, or identification obstacle; see E4, M5–M7, I3. | Explain answerability in the bridge or empirical overview, wherever the reader has the context needed. |
| Organize findings as an argument | Strongly illustrated by E5–E11; compactly combined in I5–I6; M8–M11 adds qualifications and synthesis. | Retain the principle; choose the sequence around the paper's actual question. |
| Separate design/mechanism paragraphs are optional | Compatible with their mixed placement; I6 embeds a credibility check within the findings. | When an alternative-explanation analysis exists and changes interpretation, it can enter the findings or interpretation. No check paragraph or additional analysis is required by the template. |
| Literature paragraph followed by contribution paragraph | A workable author preference, but E integrates positioning, M14 precedes M15–M18, and I7–I11 combines literature with contributions. | Separate paragraphs can do distinct work: positioning relates the paper to prior research; contributions explain what the findings add. Findings and interpretation may also span multiple paragraphs. |
| End with a roadmap | Present in M19 and I12; absent in E. | Preserve the user's requested roadmap, labeling it as the chosen drafting default rather than a universal finding. |

The most useful additions are **empirical answerability** and **decisive interpretive checks**. Scope and inference limits were already present in the skill and should remain. A plausible explanation must retain its uncertainty; rhetorical prominence does not turn it into an identified mechanism.

The preferred sequence remains motivation → question → approach → findings → literature → contributions → roadmap. Use it to ensure coverage and coherent progression. These three papers support flexibility in where functions meet, rather than a new fixed paragraph count. Do not convert their topics, numerical results, or specific mechanisms into drafting templates.
