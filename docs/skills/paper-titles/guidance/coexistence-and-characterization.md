# Coexistence and conceptual characterization

Use when a title overstates conflict between two concepts, or a new phenomenon
needs a recognizable conceptual identity. These are editorial procedures derived
from the user's framing goals and the [source record](../references/coexistence-and-characterization-sources.md),
not evidence that particular title forms improve publication outcomes.

## 1. Diagnose the relationship before choosing the connector

State the main message in ordinary language before searching for memorable words.
Ask whether the contribution concerns the presence of both concepts, how they
operate together, or a specific relation between them. Preserve that relation.

| Relationship | What the paper must establish | Possible wording |
|---|---|---|
| Coexistence | Both occur in the relevant unit, process, or setting; their joint presence matters to the argument. | `A and B in [domain]`; `The Coexistence of A and B` |
| Joint operation or integration | An account of how the elements operate together or are combined. | `Integrating A and B in [task]`, when integration is actually developed |
| Complementarity | One raises the return, value, or effectiveness of the other; two separate benefits are insufficient. | `Complementarities between A and B` |
| Duality | Distinguishable elements are interdependent aspects of a process, as theorized in the paper. | `A and B as a Duality`, when that framework is developed |
| Paradox | Contradictory yet interrelated elements coexist and persist, rather than merely appearing surprising. | `The A–B Paradox`, only if the paper develops that relationship |
| Tradeoff | Improving one entails sacrificing the other under the relevant conditions. | `The Tradeoff between A and B` |

These relationships are not mutually exclusive categories or a ladder of title
quality. A manuscript may develop several at different levels or horizons. Select
the relationship that organizes its contribution and retain decisive scope.

Do not infer coexistence within an organization from A occurring only in one set
of organizations and B only in another. Likewise, coexistence does not establish
equal importance, independence, compatibility in every circumstance, or synergy.
Two positive coefficients do not establish complementarity; opposite signs do
not by themselves establish a tradeoff.

Treat **and** as a substantive choice when both concepts belong in the message.
**Balancing** presupposes competing demands; **reconciling** suggests a resolution;
**without sacrificing** claims preservation of the other objective. Each needs
support. Do not make a faithful title seem stronger by adding those implications.

### Worked coexistence example

**Synthetic brief:** Fieldwork in service teams documents routine procedures and
improvised responses within the same customer encounters. The contribution is
their coexistence in everyday work. Neither mutual reinforcement nor a sacrifice
relation is established.

Recommended framing: **“Routine and Improvisation in Customer Service.”**

“The Routine–Improvisation Tradeoff” changes the message. “The Synergy of Routine
and Improvisation” makes an equally unsupported claim in the positive direction.
If the manuscript instead explains how routines enable improvisation, a title
such as “How Routines Enable Improvisation in Customer Service” becomes available.
Change the title when the contribution changes, not because a connector seems dull.

Farjoun's published stability/change example establishes a stronger interdependence
claim than simple coexistence. Its relevance is that familiar apparent opposites
can be understood together; do not import its duality theory into every paired title.

## 2. Characterize a new phenomenon through an established construct

Use a familiar construct to state what kind of thing the phenomenon is. Then
identify which features distinguish its operation and why those features matter.
This can position empirical as well as conceptual papers.

Complete four statements internally:

1. **Anchor:** “[Construct] denotes [defining role or properties].” Use its meaning
   in the relevant literature, not an attractive dictionary synonym.
2. **Fit:** “[Phenomenon] instantiates that construct because [supported features].”
   A resemblance alone may justify an analogy but not a literal classification.
3. **Difference:** “In this setting it operates through [distinctive features].”
   Distinctive does not mean unprecedented; verify any priority claim separately.
4. **Payoff:** “Those features help explain or reconsider [the paper's central
   question, process, relationship, or finding].” A new adjective is not a contribution.

Distinguish **application** (an established lens helps understand this instance)
from **extension** (the paper changes a construct's dimensions, relationships, or
scope). Both can support useful titles. Calling something “X as Y” does not itself
establish a new theory, and the manuscript must earn any extension claim.

### Worked example: AI search as an information intermediary

The user's **“AI Search as an Information Intermediary”** supplies a phenomenon
and a conceptual anchor. “As” directs attention to the role being studied rather
than treating AI search only as a product category.

**Synthetic supporting brief:** A study examines how a generative search service
mediates between information sources and users by selecting source material and
synthesizing answers with citations. It characterizes that mediation; it does not
estimate publisher revenue, user welfare, or the elimination of referrals.

| Element | Mapping supported by this brief |
|---|---|
| Phenomenon | AI search, specifically the studied generative search service. |
| Established anchor | Information intermediary: an actor or service mediating users' access to information sources. |
| Distinctive operation | Selection and synthesis of source material into answers presented within the service. |
| Conceptual payoff | Examining how intermediation organizes both access to sources and the presentation of their information. |

The short user-supplied title is viable if that characterization is the main
contribution. When the distinguishing operation should be visible immediately,
compare **“AI Search as an Information Intermediary: Source Selection and Answer
Synthesis.”** A further option is **“Answer Synthesis in AI Search: A Study of
Information Intermediation.”** These are illustrative formulations, not verified
published titles or recommendations for an unseen manuscript.

The title need not list every feature. Keep the shorter anchor when it identifies
the paper sufficiently and let the abstract explain the difference. Add a feature
when it distinguishes this paper's promise from a generic study of AI search.

Check the actual comparator rather than assuming traditional search only provides
links; do not manufacture a clean historical switch from referrals to answers. Nor does
generating an answer establish that users stop visiting sources. Such behavioral
or economic claims require the paper's own evidence. The sources document the
established intermediary concept and generative answer production separately;
their combination here is a framing inference, not a result about all AI search.

If a brief only benchmarks answer accuracy, an information-intermediation title
may promise a conceptual account the paper never develops. Prefer the measured
task, or label the characterization as requiring substantive development. “As”
is not a hedge that makes any conceptual label acceptable.

## 3. Choose between faithful alternatives

Compare a plain title, a coexistence title where relevant, and a characterization
title where relevant. Do not force all three onto every manuscript. Ask which
makes the paper's subject, inquiry, or answer most intelligible and worth reading.
The title need not disclose the whole contribution. Retain conflict when it is
central; prefer coexistence or characterization when they better express the
paper's identity. For choosing how much to disclose, see
[simple language and intellectual reach](../references/simple-language-and-intellectual-reach.md).
