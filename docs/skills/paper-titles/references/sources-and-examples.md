# Sources and published title examples

Compiled and checked on **2026-09-14**. This record separates source material,
verified publication facts, and this skill's editorial interpretation. The core
examples have journal issue dates in **2020–2025**. They are a purposive teaching
sample, not a systematic review or a ranking of titles.

## What was integrated from the requested repositories

The skill is an original synthesis; it does not bundle or execute upstream scripts.
The links below pin the files inspected to their repository commits so that later
upstream changes do not silently change this provenance record.

## Conceptual grounding for problematization

Mats Alvesson and Jörgen Sandberg (2011), “Generating Research Questions Through
Problematization,” *Academy of Management Review* 36(2), 247–271.
[Publisher record](https://journals.aom.org/doi/abs/10.5465/amr.2009.0188);
[accessible institutional record and abstract](https://lup.lub.lu.se/record/1965150).

Their abstract distinguishes identifying a gap from examining the assumptions that
organize an existing body of theory. This skill adapts that distinction to help
locate a paper's motivating tension. It does not infer that every good title must
challenge a theory, or that this article establishes an effect of title wording on
citations. This older conceptual source supplements the recent title examples.

## Economics examples

Titles, authors, and substantive summaries below were checked against publisher
records or published copies hosted by the authors; article metadata was also
cross-checked against Crossref. Title footnote markers are omitted. **Title reading** is our
interpretation of the wording, not a claim about the authors' intentions. Read each
**boundary** before borrowing the structure.

### 1. A Few Bad Apples? Racial Bias in Policing

Felipe Goncalves and Steven Mello. **AER, 2021**, 111(5), 1406–1441.
[Published article and abstract](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20181607).

- **Paper:** Uses Florida Highway Patrol speeding-ticket data and a bunching design
  to estimate discrimination and its distribution across officers.
- **Title reading:** A familiar explanation becomes a testable question; the second
  clause identifies the subject in searchable language.
- **Boundary:** The hook works because the paper examines the distribution across
  officers. A study reporting only average disparities could not promise the same
  assessment, and the setting does not represent every police organization.

The [AEA's author interview](https://www.aeaweb.org/research/racial-bias-policing-florida)
also discusses the explanation behind the question.

### 2. When Product Markets Become Collective Traps: The Case of Social Media

Leonardo Bursztyn, Benjamin Handel, Rafael Jiménez-Durán, and Christopher Roth.
**AER, 2025**, 115(12), 4105–4136.
[Published article and abstract](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20231468).

- **Paper:** Incentivized experiments with college students on TikTok and Instagram
  examine welfare when platforms impose costs on nonusers as well as users.
- **Title reading:** The conceptual phrase names a conflict between individual
  participation and collective desirability; the subtitle supplies the application.
- **Boundary:** “When” is conditional. Borrowing the form requires identifying the
  conditions or forces that create the problem, not asserting that all markets or
  all social-media use are harmful.

Use the published 2025 version when citing this example, not the 2023 working-paper
date. It is included for its instructive framing, not a verified influence ranking.

### 3. The Fall of the Labor Share and the Rise of Superstar Firms

David Autor, David Dorn, Lawrence F. Katz, Christina Patterson, and John Van Reenen.
**QJE, 2020**, 135(2), 645–709.
[Publisher record](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/135/2/645/5721266);
[published PDF at MIT](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/Autor%20et%20al_2020_The%20Fall%20of%20the%20Labor%20Share%20and%20t.pdf).

- **Paper:** Develops a firm-reallocation explanation for falling labor shares and
  evaluates its predictions using US and international evidence.
- **Title reading:** Parallel movements make the explanatory puzzle memorable,
  while a concrete name identifies the proposed explanation.
- **Boundary:** The paper combines a model and supporting empirical patterns. The
  connecting word “and” is not a license to imply an independently identified,
  universal causal effect in a new paper.

### 4. The Welfare Effects of Social Media

Hunt Allcott, Luca Braghieri, Sarah Eichmeyer, and Matthew Gentzkow.
**AER, 2020**, 110(3), 629–676.
[Published article and abstract](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20190658).

- **Paper:** Randomized Facebook deactivation before the 2018 US midterms changes
  several outcomes, including well-being, polarization, and factual news knowledge.
- **Title reading:** An economic quantity organizes competing benefits and costs
  without forcing them into a one-direction verdict.
- **Boundary:** This is a useful descriptive baseline, not permission to omit
  material platform, population, or time restrictions from a new title. Welfare
  language needs an actual welfare analysis, beyond an engagement outcome alone.

### 5. Generative AI at Work

Erik Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li, and Lindsey Raymond.
**QJE, 2025**, 140(2), 889–942.
[Publisher record](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658);
[published PDF hosted by Danielle Li](https://danielle.li/assets/docs/GenerativeAIatWork.pdf).

- **Paper:** Studies deployment of generative-AI assistance in customer support,
  with productivity effects that differ across experience and skill groups.
- **Title reading:** A recognizable technology and work setting carry the title in
  four words. A conspicuous paradox is unnecessary.
- **Boundary:** It does not establish effects on every occupation, wages, job
  creation, or long-run employment. Its breadth should not override a new paper's
  need for a more specific outcome and setting.

The journal article appeared online and in an issue in 2025. Earlier working-paper
dates and estimates should not be combined with the published version.

## Management and marketing examples

### 6. Close Enough? A Large-Scale Exploration of Non-Experimental Approaches to Advertising Measurement

Brett R. Gordon, Robert Moakler, and Florian Zettelmeyer.
**Marketing Science, 2023**, 42(4), 768–793; first online in 2022.
[Published article and abstract](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2022.1413).

- **Paper:** Compares two observational measurement approaches with results from
  663 Facebook experiments.
- **Title reading:** The brief question makes approximation to an experimental
  benchmark the problem; the subtitle names the approaches and application.
- **Boundary:** The paper evaluates specific methods and data. The title does not
  support a general assertion that every nonexperimental method fails.

The publisher abstract supplies the study count; an author webpage inspected during
compilation displayed a conflicting count and was not used for that detail.

### 7. Valuing Intrinsic and Instrumental Preferences for Privacy

Tesary Lin. **Marketing Science, 2022**, 41(4), 663–681.
[Published article and abstract](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2022.1368).

- **Paper:** Combines an experiment and a structural model to distinguish preferences
  for privacy itself from preferences arising from consequences of disclosure.
- **Title reading:** A consequential distinction supplies the intellectual interest;
  no slogan or question mark is necessary.
- **Boundary:** “Valuing” reflects a measurement contribution. It does not promise
  one universal monetary value of privacy across settings and people.

### 8. Welfare Effects of Personalized Rankings

Robert Donnelly, Ayush Kanodia, and Ilya Morozov.
**Marketing Science, 2024**, 43(1), 92–113; first online in 2023.
[Published article and abstract](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2023.1441).

- **Paper:** Combines a retailer experiment and a search model to study personalized
  rankings and their welfare consequences.
- **Title reading:** The intervention and evaluation criterion identify the paper;
  the underlying tension concerns consumer matching and firms' incentives.
- **Boundary:** Observed purchases are not directly observed welfare. Model-based
  welfare conclusions should remain distinct from experimental behavioral outcomes.

### 9. Trust and Disintermediation: Evidence from an Online Freelance Marketplace

Grace Gu and Feng Zhu. **Management Science, 2021**, 67(2), 794–807;
first online in 2020.
[Published article and abstract](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2020.3583).

- **Paper:** Examines how greater trust can increase hiring while encouraging
  off-platform transactions when trust is sufficiently high.
- **Title reading:** A valued input and an adverse platform consequence expose an
  incentive conflict. The subtitle gives the relationship an institutional setting.
- **Boundary:** The conclusion is not that trust is universally harmful or that the
  platform's total profits must decline.

### 10. Reducing Discrimination with Reviews in the Sharing Economy: Evidence from Field Experiments on Airbnb

Ruomeng Cui, Jun Li, and Dennis J. Zhang.
**Management Science, 2020**, 66(3), 1071–1094; first online in 2019.
[Published article and abstract](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2018.3273).

- **Paper:** Uses randomized field experiments to study reviews and discrimination
  in hosts' responses to accommodation requests.
- **Title reading:** Problem, intervention, design, and recognizable setting each
  earn their words; length serves the evidentiary promise.
- **Boundary:** “Reducing” is not “eliminating.” The intervention claim does not extend
  to every form of discrimination or every sharing-economy transaction.

### 11. Targeting for Long-Term Outcomes

Jeremy Yang, Dean Eckles, Paramveer Dhillon, and Sinan Aral.
**Management Science, 2024**, 70(6), 3841–3855; first online in 2023.
[Published article and abstract](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2023.4881).

- **Paper:** Combines surrogacy theory, policy learning, and newspaper field
  experiments to support targeting decisions for outcomes observed later.
- **Title reading:** The task and time horizon expose the constraint: decisions
  precede observation of the desired outcome.
- **Boundary:** Borrow the task-and-constraint structure, not a promise of guaranteed
  long-run success. Inferences require the assumptions linking earlier information
  to later outcomes.

## Maintaining the reference

When refreshing examples, verify the exact published title, authors, journal, issue
year, DOI, and the abstract or relevant full text. Record online-first dates when
they differ. Distinguish a working paper from its published version. Use original
publisher, author, institution, or awarding-body sources; inspect any conflicting
metadata before choosing a version.

For an added example, record the substantive contribution, the title's function,
its evidentiary boundary, and any claimed recognition with a dated source. Label
craft judgments as interpretations. Do not invent awards, count every paper in a
prestigious journal as influential, or convert these examples into universal rules
about punctuation, length, or publication success.
