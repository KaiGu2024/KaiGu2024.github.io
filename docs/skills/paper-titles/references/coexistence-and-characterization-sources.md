# Sources for coexistence and conceptual characterization

Checked **2026-09-30**. This is a focused source selection, not a systematic review.
The sources ground conceptual distinctions; none tests the relative performance
of title wording. Operational advice and invented titles are in the
[guide](../guidance/coexistence-and-characterization.md).

## Coexistence, interdependence, and complementarity

**Moshe Farjoun (2010). “Beyond Dualism: Stability and Change As a Duality.”**
*Academy of Management Review*, 35(2), 202–225.
[Publisher abstract](https://journals.aom.org/doi/abs/10.5465/amr.35.2.zok202).

Locator: abstract. The paper develops stability and change as interdependent,
including mutually enabling relations, rather than simply incompatible. This
provides a published example of a title whose paired concepts support an account
of their connection. It does not establish that all coexisting concepts reinforce
one another. The issue date is April 2010; the publisher also displays a later
2017 online date.

**Paul Milgrom and John Roberts (1995). “Complementarities and Fit: Strategy,
Structure, and Organizational Change in Manufacturing.”** *Journal of Accounting
and Economics*, 19, 179–208.
[Author-hosted published PDF](https://milgrom.people.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/1995/05/Complementarities-and-fit.pdf).

Locator: abstract and section 2, printed p. 181. Complementarity concerns an
increase in one activity raising returns to another. This is a stronger relation
than practices appearing together or each having a beneficial association.

**Wendy K. Smith and Marianne W. Lewis (2011). “Toward a Theory of Paradox:
A Dynamic Equilibrium Model of Organizing.”** *Academy of Management Review*,
36(2), 381–403.
[Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.2009.0223);
[university-hosted full text](https://udspace.udel.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/fcc8b283-9ae0-462c-b416-fd6514b5b13c/content).

Locator: definition on printed p. 382 and elaboration on p. 386. Paradox involves
contradiction and interrelation with simultaneous, persistent existence. The
concept therefore requires more than two observations occurring together.

## Established concepts and new features

**Elina Jaakkola (2020). “Designing Conceptual Articles: Four Approaches.”**
*AMS Review*, 10, 18–26.
[Open publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13162-020-00161-0).

Locators: “Theory adaptation” and “Typology.” Theory adaptation revises a conceptual
perspective with help from other theories; typologies distinguish variants along
justified dimensions. These support separating an application of an existing
construct from a substantive extension or differentiation. The guide's four-part
title check is our editorial adaptation, not Jaakkola's titling method. A title
using “as” alone does not meet her requirements for theory adaptation.

**Andrei Hagiu and Bruno Jullien (2011). “Why Do Intermediaries Divert Search?”**
*The RAND Journal of Economics*, 42(2), 337–362.
[Publisher abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1756-2171.2011.00136.x).

Locator: abstract. The model studies an information intermediary connecting
consumer search with affiliated sellers and analyzes incentives affecting search.
It anchors intermediation as an established economic role. It is neither a study
of generative search nor evidence that its particular incentives apply to every
AI service.

**Nelson Liu, Tianyi Zhang, and Percy Liang (2023). “Evaluating Verifiability in
Generative Search Engines.”** *Findings of EMNLP 2023*, 7001–7025.
[Publisher abstract and paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.467/).

Locator: abstract. The paper describes systems generating answers with inline
citations and audits their verifiability. This grounds answer generation and
source attribution as concrete features to consider in an AI-search
characterization. It does not identify publisher-revenue or referral effects,
and its audited systems do not establish current performance for all services.

## Scope of the synthesis

“AI Search as an Information Intermediary” is the user's illustrative title,
not a published title verified here. Connecting that conceptual anchor to answer
synthesis is an inference from the two preceding sources and the supplied idea.
A specific manuscript must establish its own mapping and central contribution.
